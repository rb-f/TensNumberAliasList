Basic Numbers (0–10)

1 — Ordinary Number

I call one as an "ordinary number". Why did I call this "ordinary"? It is so general that learning this number is very important. Counting objects requires adding one. You live as one. When you divide a number by itself, you get one. And hundreds of more things related to one. This is why in my The Zoo of Numbers, he is really general and has no clear exaggeration.

4 — First Darwin Number

4 is what I call as the 1st "Darwin Number". This actually stems from a Season 3 episode from The Amazing World of Gumball (which was my favorite show as a kid) named "The Mothers", where a competition of three mothers (of Gumball, Banana Joe and Tobias) affects on how they try to become one of the "best" mothers of the world. During the competition Darwin holds cards with numbers of the rounds written on it. There are ten moments where he did that. The number 4 is the smallest number that appears here, but the next one is exactly eight times bigger.

7 — Sompoh/Heckven

Sompoh is one of my satirical invented words of the number 7 in 2025, meant to replace the "different" seven in Indonesian. I since did not use it anymore, but nevertheless I still loved it. This stems from seven-segment display. Normally the number 7 is written with three line segments: top, top-right and bottom-right. However, on some displays, especially from Japan the number 7 has an extra segment at the top-left. While it's technically still seven I did some sort of satire. Imagine a language where changing the glyph style of the same letter influences the meaning. In Indonesian 7 is "tujuh", but I came up with a random name "sompoh" for the number 7 with that heck extra segment at the top-left. In English I named it "heckven" (too similar to heaven...?). That's why I made cursed names like saying "heckven thousand zero" for the number 70,000 while seeing it in gas stations.

18 — Argton/Octon

I sometimes call the number 18 as "Argton" because of some sort of linguistic coincidence I noticed, which is the Swedish word for 18 and the 18th element in the periodic table. 18 in Swedish is "arton", and the 18th element is "argon". Notice there is only one difference in the letter, which is the third letter. How can that happen?

Fun fact: I made this name while building the list. Before this was made I usually said it as "octon", erroneously extrapolated from "sexton" which is 16 in Swedish.

400 — First D-Number

The number 400 is what I call as the first "D-number". A D-number is a three-digit number where the Roman numeral representation contains a D. That is, a D-number is a number between 400 and 899.

This stems from a problem regarding numbers from a children's TV show named Numberblocks (which is what I loved to watch), a world full of blocky characters representing numbers. In the episode "One Thousand and One", there are several characters (currently) exclusive to this episode, which are the numbers 200, 300 and 900.

The problem is: how do we make any number using officially-rigged Numberblocks? This question assumes that using compounds are allowed, but no fanmade Numberblocks. A fanmade Numberblock is a Numberblock that is not officially in the show. Regarding hundreds, only 100, 200, 300 and 900 are official hundred characters in Numberblocks. The rest are unofficial and only fanmades exist. Assuming using that question, from 100 to 999, we can make the Numberblocks 100-399 as well as 900-999. We cannot make 400-899 using purely official Numberblocks, since each hundred will be likely a new rig if they were official rigs. Therefore no D-numbers can made using this assumption.

However, at the same time due to the fact of the digit 7 being written with four line segments, I did say it as "heckven thousand zero" (English literation), which looks cursed. 34,521,352 — Melody Number

This is what I call as the "Melody Number". This is because of something that could be used as a melody. By mapping each of the digits into a musical note (1 = C, 2 = D, 3 = E, 4 = F, 5 = G), and then playing them one by one, you get a melody. Fun fact: I named recently it in 2026, the first time I found it interesting was in 2023, when testing a Minecraft seed with that number and found a bug with the world generation.
